## Where it started

When I joined Digital Auxilius as Technical Team Lead in October 2024, the main product was about a year and a half behind schedule. The team had more than 19 engineers across frontend, backend, mobile, QA and DevOps, and the product still wasn't shipping.

A delay that long is rarely one problem. So before changing anything, I looked for where the time was actually going.

## Finding the causes

I audited the codebases and the development process together, from how work was planned to how it reached a release.

I found four problems feeding each other. The timelines weren't realistic, nobody clearly owned what, the code slowed every change down, and management and engineering kept pulling against each other.

## What changed

- **A plan people could believe.** I re-planned the roadmap around what the team could really deliver, and ran it with Agile practices so progress showed up every sprint.
- **Ownership.** Each part of the process had a named owner, so problems stopped falling between teams.
- **Code quality standards.** Every merge had to pass review rules and quality checks, so new work stopped making old problems worse.
- **Incentives that matched delivery.** Performance-linked compensation, so the team's reward was tied to what shipped.

## Where it ended up

The delayed product was delivered within four months of the new plan. Code quality and team discipline went up with it, and management and engineering stopped pulling in different directions.

## What I'd tell a team in the same spot

Don't start with a rewrite or a reorganisation. Find out where the weeks are actually going first. Here the fixes were smaller than the delay made them look, and the new plan only worked once the team believed it.
